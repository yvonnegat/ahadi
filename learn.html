Know your rights. Read the record.

Ahadi works because Kenya's Constitution already gives you the right to this information — the register just makes it usable. This page explains what a case file means, where that right comes from, and how to take part with or without a smartphone.

What Ahadi is — and isn't

Ahadi connects four things that are normally scattered across different offices: what was promised, what was funded, what was supposed to happen, and what residents report is actually happening. It never accuses anyone of theft or corruption — it flags a potential discrepancy when the official record and community reports disagree, and leaves the conclusion to you, the press, or an oversight body.

Ahadi is not a budget portal, not a list of projects, and not a place to "report your corrupt politician." It's a record of promises, evidence, and the gap between them — nothing more, nothing less.

Where this right comes from

General civic information, not legal advice. If you need advice on a specific situation, consult a lawyer or a legal aid organisation.

Constitution of Kenya, 2010 — Article 1

Sovereignty belongs to the people. All state authority is exercised on the people's behalf — the starting premise for why the public is entitled to know how public power and public money are used.

Article 10

National values and principles of governance bind every public officer and body, and include democracy, participation of the people, transparency, and accountability.

Article 35

Right to information. Every citizen has the right to access information held by the state, and information held by another person where it's needed to protect a right.

Articles 174 & 175

Devolution exists partly to give people a greater say in decisions that affect them and to ensure county governments involve communities in their own affairs — the constitutional basis for county-level public participation.

Article 201

Principles of public finance require openness, accountability, and public participation in financial matters, and that public money is used prudently and responsibly.

Article 232

Values and principles of public service include transparency, and the timely provision of accurate information to the public.

How to read a case file

Every promise on the register is laid out as a three-clause record — open any case file and you'll see:

I. Promised
What was said, by whom, and by when — usually sourced from a speech, manifesto, or development plan.
II. Funded
The budget allocated, the funding source, and the status the government has officially reported.
III. Reality
What residents are reporting on the ground, tallied by category, with any discrepancy or delay flagged automatically.

Glossary

Case code
A short reference like P001, printed on every case file so it's easy to look up over SMS or USSD without typing a long ID.
Official status
The completion status the implementing government body has reported — not independently verified by Ahadi.
Community report
A first-hand observation submitted by a resident: what they've seen, when, and where. Anonymous reports are allowed.
Discrepancy flag
A neutral notice shown when the official status and recent community reports point in different directions. It names the disagreement — it does not assign blame.
Community pattern
Shown when ten or more independent reports agree on the same thing — a signal that's harder to dismiss as one person's complaint.

Using Ahadi without data or a smartphone

Every feature on the web register is also available by USSD or SMS.

USSD — dial in

*XXX#

  1. My area — pick your ward and see the summary
  2. View promises — browse tracked projects near you
  3. Check project — look up a case by its code
  4. Report progress — submit what you're seeing
  5. Follow project — get an SMS when it's updated
SMS — text a command

Once you know a case code (from a flyer, a neighbour, or the USSD menu), text any of these:

  • REPORT P001 3 — report a status (1=not started, 2=ongoing, 3=stalled, 4=complete, 5=doesn't match)
  • FOLLOW P001 — get notified when this case changes
  • UNFOLLOW P001 — stop notifications
  • STATUS P001 — get a quick summary by return SMS
